Live · Installable · Ad-Free

Track trains & flights

A calm, ad-free live tracker for Indian trains and flights. Your stop is the hero, not the train.

Every train tracker I used fired 266 requests and megabytes of ads to answer one question: where is my train? Flight apps swing the other way and bury you in telemetry you never asked for.

So I built Track around one idea: show what matters to the traveller right now, and nothing else. The hero is your stop, not the train's raw position. Flights reshape themselves by phase - the gate leads before takeoff, the map in the air, the baggage belt after landing.

Under the hood it's a platform, not a page. One Cloudflare Worker normalises the running feed, NTES, IRCTC seats, AeroDataBox and live ADS-B into one shape, so the frontend never knows who answered. A free-tier flight quota stretches across every user through shared caches and a daily budget. And stale data is labelled, never passed off as exact.

Live · Open Source · Free Forever

Decoding Credit Cards

22 interactive chapters on how credit cards actually work. No ads, no pitches, no card recommendations.

I wanted to understand how credit cards actually work - not "best card for lounge access" content, the real mechanics. How a transaction clears. What the bank does when you swipe. Why the minimum payment is a trap.

There was no clean source for any of it. Just me with scattered tabs, RBI documents, and late-night YouTube rabbit holes. So I built the thing I wished existed: 22 interactive chapters where you tap a card to watch a transaction settle, drag a slider to watch compound interest destroy you in real time.

Built end-to-end - design system, content, and code - in pure HTML, CSS, and JS. No build step, no frameworks, zero dependencies. Free for everyone forever. No login, no ads, no card recommendations, ever.

Product Teardown · Working Prototype

Jira 2.0

A teardown and working prototype of an AI-native layer on Jira, where every AI output is auditable.

Jira is the most-used PM tool on the planet, and it still asks every user to low-key be a database admin. Configure a filter, save it, build a dashboard, wire up a gadget - all to answer a question that took ten seconds to ask.

So I tore it down and rebuilt the interaction layer. Not a Jira clone. An AI-native layer on top of the system of record - same data, same workflows, same integrations. It just stops taxing your attention before delivering value.

Three surfaces:
• an action-first home that answers what blocks others, what blocks me, what's overdue.
• Conversational ticket creation, maker-checker preserved.
• And natural-language dashboards that write the JQL for you.

The differentiator is trust - every AI output is auditable. Click any suggestion and a drawer opens: signals used, plain-English reasoning, a confidence number, and the cost of being wrong.

The Cookbook by Parth

216 high-protein dishes that start from context: what's in the fridge, how long you've got, who's coming.

Recipe sites are built for search engines, not hungry people. A life story, three ads, and the ingredient list somewhere below the fold. None of them answer the real question: what should I cook tonight?

So mine starts from context, not chapters. I have 20 minutes. I have chicken. Zero cleanup. Someone's coming over. Tap what's in the fridge and it ranks dishes with honest set math; pick one and cook mode takes over the screen, timers running and the screen kept awake.

Underneath, it's a data product: 216 cards, an ingredient database, 421 aliases, and a graph of pairings and leftovers. A validator blocks the deploy if any card's macros disagree with the database. Nothing is estimated.
